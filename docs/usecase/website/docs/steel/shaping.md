# How Metals Are Given Their Final Shape

Once a metal has been produced and refined, it must be **shaped into usable
products**. The method depends on:

- The desired object
- The metal's properties
- The required strength
- The production volume

Some techniques shape **molten** metal, while others deform **solid** metal
under heat or pressure.

## Shaping techniques

### Casting

**Molten metal is poured into a mold** and allowed to solidify. This is useful
for complex shapes such as engine blocks, valves, or machine parts.

### Rolling

**Hot or cold metal is passed between large rollers** to reduce thickness or
create sheets, plates, rails, beams, or strips.

### Forging

**Metal is compressed, hammered, or pressed into shape.** This improves strength
and is used for tools, crankshafts, gears, and high-stress components.

### Extrusion

**Metal is forced through a shaped die** to create long products with a constant
cross-section, such as tubes, rods, and aluminum profiles.

### Drawing

**Metal is pulled through a die** to reduce its diameter, commonly used for
wires, cables, and tubes.

### Machining

**Material is removed by cutting, drilling, milling, or turning** to achieve
precise dimensions and surface finishes.

## At a glance

| Technique | What happens | Typical products |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Casting** | Molten metal poured into a mold | Engine blocks, valves, machine parts |
| **Rolling** | Metal passed between rollers | Sheets, plates, rails, beams, strips |
| **Forging** | Metal hammered or pressed | Tools, crankshafts, gears |
| **Extrusion** | Metal forced through a die | Tubes, rods, aluminum profiles |
| **Drawing** | Metal pulled through a die | Wires, cables, tubes |
| **Machining** | Material removed by cutting | Precise, finished components |
