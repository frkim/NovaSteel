# How Iron and Steel Are Produced in Factories

There are two main modern routes for making steel: the **blast furnace / basic
oxygen furnace** route and the **electric arc furnace** route. NovaSteel
operates both.

## The blast furnace route

One traditional way to make steel starts with a **blast furnace**. In this
route, **iron ore, coke, and limestone** are charged into a very tall furnace.

Coke — a carbon-rich fuel made from coal — provides both the **heat** and the
**chemical reaction** needed to remove oxygen from the iron ore. The furnace
operates at extremely high temperatures, producing:

- **Molten iron**, also called **hot metal** or **pig iron**
- A separate waste layer called **slag**

## The basic oxygen furnace

Molten iron from the blast furnace contains **too much carbon** to be used as
steel. It is therefore transferred into a **basic oxygen furnace**, also called
a **converter**.

Pure **oxygen** is blown onto the molten metal, burning off excess carbon and
reducing unwanted impurities. At this stage:

- **Scrap steel** can be added
- **Alloying elements** such as chromium, nickel, or manganese may be introduced
  to create specific grades of steel

!!! note "From iron to steel"
    The blast furnace makes **iron**. The basic oxygen furnace turns that iron
    into **steel** by removing most of the carbon.

## The electric arc furnace route

Another major route is the **electric arc furnace (EAF)**. Instead of starting
mainly from iron ore, this process usually melts **recycled scrap steel** using
powerful electric arcs generated by **graphite electrodes**.

The molten steel is then refined and adjusted to the required composition. This
route is widely used because it:

- Supports **recycling**
- Is **flexible** for producing different grades of steel

## Comparing the two routes

| | Blast furnace / BOF | Electric arc furnace |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Main input** | Iron ore, coke, limestone | Recycled scrap steel |
| **Energy source** | Coke (chemical + heat) | Electricity |
| **Key strength** | Large-scale primary production | Recycling and flexibility |
